- **Recognition:**
  - **18 of its projects** have been named national **"Intelligent Manufacturing Demonstration Factories"** by China's Ministry of Industry and IT (MIIT).
  - National "Little Giant" specialised enterprise.
  - ISO 9001, 14001 and 45001 certified.
- **Position in transformers:** AIMS states that it has delivered **more than half of China's automation projects** in the transformer and power-equipment sector.
- **Export experience:** projects delivered in Thailand (Eaglerise, a transformer manufacturer), Taiwan and Vietnam.

## 3. What AIMS does: three areas

1. **Internal logistics:** automated storage and retrieval systems (AS/RS), RGVs (rail-guided vehicles), AGVs, conveyors, automatic loading and unloading, and the warehouse and logistics software (WMS, WCS, LES, uRMS).
2. **Production operations:** manufacturing execution system (MES), production planning, robotic workstations and vision inspection.
3. **Process and energy optimisation:** AI-based control of furnaces and process lines that improves quality and reduces energy use.

All three are connected by AIMS's own platform, **AIMS-MOS**. AIMS develops all of its software itself and sells it **per module, with no limit on the number of users**.

## 4. What AIMS can do in a transformer factory

AIMS can automate plants for **both oil-type and dry-type transformers**. Typical scope:
- **Raw materials:** automated storage for silicon-steel coils, copper and aluminium foil and wire, with automatic loading and unloading, including storage around slitting machines.
- **Core:** an intelligent core-nesting algorithm and core-insertion stations.
- **Coils:** coil-shape inspection and automatic recycling of empty spools.
- **Assembly:** robotic press-fit and grinding workstations, plus final and body assembly stations.
- **Drying and tanking:** RGV lines for body assembly, drying and transfer between workshops.
- **Finished goods:** automated storage and dispatch.
- **Software:** WMS and MES, giving every transformer a full production record (traceability), which is increasingly required by customers in Europe and the US.

**Reference: TBEA, Tianjin (China)**
- AIMS delivered all phases across **four plants**, from 2020 to today.
- Its scope covered everything except the process machines themselves (such as slitting and drying machines).
- Results reported by AIMS:
  - **+30% production efficiency;**
  - **–50% production cycle time;**
  - **+30% capacity;**
  - **+20% inventory turnover.**

**Reference: Eaglerise, Thailand.** An export project for a transformer manufacturer, from contract to final acceptance in about **12 months**. Scope: core insertion, assembly stations, RGV body assembly and drying lines, and workshop transfer.

## 5. How a project works (and why it is low-risk to start)

1. **Online meeting:** AIMS's sales and solution engineers, together with Ulusoy, learn about the plant: products, volumes, layout and pain points.
2. **Site visit:** to collect data.
3. **Free concept and feasibility study:** AIMS prepares layout drawings, calculations and the key figures for investors, **free of charge**. This takes 2 weeks for a simple project and about 1 month for a complex one, refined over several meetings.
4. **Approval:** the customer approves the final concept. AIMS then delivers the bill of quantities and final price within a few days.
5. **Implementation:** AIMS's project managers lead the suppliers and the local installation team. Installation typically takes **2–6 months**.
6. **Acceptance, training and after-sales service:** Ulusoy supports locally and AIMS supports remotely and on site.

For a **new factory**, AIMS works with the building contractor so that the automation fits the hall from the first drawing. For an **existing factory**, the concept is designed around current production.

## 6. Why it matters for a Turkish transformer maker

- **Capacity without new halls:** automated storage builds upward and frees floor space.
- **Shorter lead times:** this matters while transformer demand and export orders are strong.
- **Traceability for export customers** in the EU and US.
- **Lower dependence on manual handling and rising labour costs.**
- **Safer handling of heavy parts:** cores, coils, active parts and tanks.
- **A partner that has done it at scale:** TBEA is one of the world's largest transformer makers.

## 8. Questions to ask the transformer company (to qualify the opportunity)

1. Which products do you make (oil or dry type, distribution or power, size range), and in what annual volumes?
2. Are you planning a **new factory, a new hall or an expansion**? When?
3. Where are your biggest problems today: storage space, material handling, heavy-part movement, lead times, traceability, labour?
4. How do you store raw materials (steel coils, foil, wire) and finished products today?
5. Do you use an MES or WMS, and which ERP system (e.g. SAP)?
6. Who would decide on an automation investment, and what is the expected budget and timeline?
7. Could you share a layout drawing and basic data, so AIMS can prepare a **free concept**?

## 9. Things to avoid

- **Don't quote prices.** Every project is priced after the concept.
- **Present results as figures reported by AIMS,** not guarantees.
- **Don't promise the energy-saving figures** for the furnace software yet; they still need to be verified.
- **Don't mention AIMS's revenue** or internal figures.
