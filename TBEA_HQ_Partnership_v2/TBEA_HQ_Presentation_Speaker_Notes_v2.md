# TBEA HQ Presentation — Speaker Script (v2)

**Deck:** Ulusoy - TBEA Partnership (57 slides, including 5 short section dividers)
**Audience:** TBEA senior management and business-unit executives
**Target length:** 60–75 minutes of presenting plus discussion. Dividers take about 10 seconds each.
**Language:** Speak slowly and simply. Many listeners are non-native English speakers, and some may be following through an interpreter. Pause after every number.

**If time is cut to 40 minutes, use this short path:** 1–4, 7, 12, 14–17, 19, 21, 24, 26–29, 31–33, 36, 38, 40, 42, 44, 45, 47, 50–52, 54, 56, 57. Skip the others and refer to them as "in your copy". Slides 19, 22 and 23 each have a one-sentence skim version.

**Three rules for the whole meeting**
1. Never say or imply that TBEA has been selected, approved or ordered on any project. The phrase to use is "potential TBEA supply scope".
2. Do not name individual decision-makers or private accounts in Türkiye. Named accounts are shared after the registration mechanism is agreed.
3. Do not discuss commission, margins or exclusivity terms in this meeting. The aim is a contact person and a working session.
4. Keep the tone factual and modest. Let the facts speak; avoid superlatives and pressure.

## Slide 19 — Demand is at record levels — and set to almost double by 2040

**Main message:** Türkiye's electricity demand hit a record in 2025 and the official target is 680 TWh by 2040.

**Script:**
"Türkiye consumed a record 361 terawatt-hours in 2025, with a record peak of almost 60 gigawatts. On 5 November, a few days ago, the Energy Minister presented the new national strategy for 2040. Demand is planned to rise to 680 terawatt-hours, almost double today. Installed capacity is planned to rise from 123 to 281 gigawatts. Behind every one of these gigawatts is a transformer, a switchgear bay and a cable."

**Emphasise:** "Five November, a few days ago": the information is fresh.
**Detailed talking points (major slide):**
- The 2030 and 2035 bars come from the earlier National Energy Plan; the 2040 bar is the new Milli Enerji 2040 target.
- About 90 TWh of 2040 demand is electric vehicles; electrification is the main driver.
- The strategy also includes 10 GW of nuclear, which is outside TBEA's core scope.

**Likely TBEA question:** "Are these targets realistic? Turkish plans are often optimistic."
**Suggested response:** "That is a fair challenge. Even the current-policies scenario in the same strategy reaches about 550 TWh by 2040, which is still a 50% increase. And the grid investment we show next is needed even at the lower scenario. We do not build our case on the highest number."

**If short of time (skim):** "Demand reached a record 361 terawatt-hours in 2025, and the new national strategy targets 680 by 2040, almost double."

**Transition:** "Where will this new capacity come from?"

## Slide 20 — 127 GW installed — and wind + solar must almost triple by 2035

**Main message:** Wind and solar must grow from about 44 GW to 120 GW by 2035, which means 8–10 GW a year.

**Script:**
"Türkiye has 127 gigawatts installed today. Solar is now 28 gigawatts, more than natural gas. The official target is 120 gigawatts of wind and solar by 2035, against about 44 today. To reach it, Türkiye must add 8 to 10 gigawatts every year. The record so far is about 6.5. So the pace must increase, and every additional gigawatt needs step-up transformers, switchgear, cables and a grid connection."

**Detailed talking points:**
- In the YEKA auctions of November 2026, 1,500 MW of wind and 785 MW of solar were awarded. The government has committed to at least 2 GW of auctions a year.
- An offshore wind auction is planned for 2027.

**Likely TBEA question:** "Who supplies the inverters and transformers for these plants today?"
**Suggested response:** "A mix of Turkish manufacturers, European groups and Chinese suppliers. Inverters are already heavily Chinese. Transformers and high-voltage switchgear are more contested, and local factories are full. We will come back to this on the competition slide."

**Transition:** "Generation alone is not enough. The grid must grow too."

## Slide 21 — The grid must grow as fast as generation

**Main message:** $28bn of transmission investment to 2035, HVDC planned, and transformer capacity rising by about 58% by 2040.

**Script:**
"The government plans 28 billion dollars of transmission investment by 2035. That includes 15,000 kilometres of new AC lines and about 9,600 kilometres of HVDC corridors. The regulator has approved about 18.5 billion dollars of distribution investment for 2026 to 2030. By 2040, high-voltage lines grow from 77,000 to 112,000 kilometres, and transformer capacity rises by almost 60%. TEİAŞ operates about 820 substations today. Many more will be built and modernised."

**Detailed talking points (major slide):**
- HVDC is at the specification stage. TEİAŞ has commissioned consultancy work for a ±525 kV, 2 GW design. No construction tender has been issued yet.
- TEİAŞ signed an HVDC memorandum with Hitachi Energy at COP31 in Antalya last week. Say this neutrally; TBEA will know it.
- The HVDC corridor figure was revised from 14,700 km (2024) to about 9,580 km (2026). If challenged, say we use the latest official figure.

**Likely TBEA question:** "Can TBEA participate in the HVDC programme?"
**Suggested response:** "We see HVDC as a long-term positioning goal, not a first-year target. TBEA's UHV and converter-transformer references are world-class. The right step now is to be known to TEİAŞ and its consultants before the specification is fixed. That is something we can help prepare."

**Transition:** "The third driver is storage, where the pipeline is large but has barely started."

## Slide 22 — Storage: a 33 GW licensed pipeline, barely started

**Main message:** About 33 GW of storage-linked licences exist, but only about 0.2 GW is installed. Equipment demand is still ahead.

**Script:**
"In 2022 and 2023 Türkiye gave licences for wind and solar plants with integrated storage. The rule is that storage capacity must equal the plant's capacity. About 33 gigawatts of these licences exist, more than in any EU country. But in early 2026 only about 0.2 gigawatts was installed. The government expects 2 to 3 gigawatts to come online from 2026, and the target is 140 gigawatt-hours by 2040. New regulations this year also allow storage in industrial zones and at consumer sites."

**Emphasise:** The gap between 33 GW licensed and 0.2 GW installed.

**Likely TBEA question:** "Will all 33 GW really be built?"
**Suggested response:** "No, certainly not all of it. Many licences will be delayed or cancelled. But even a fraction is several gigawatts of PCS, battery systems, MV skids and transformers. And it is a private market, where price, delivery and bankability matter more than domestic preference."

**If short of time (skim):** "About 33 gigawatts of storage-linked licences exist, but only around 0.2 gigawatts is built. Most of the equipment demand is still ahead."

**Transition:** "Two more new sources of demand are data centers and industry."

## Slide 28 — Private 154 kV connection substations for renewables and storage

**Main message:** Every new renewable or hybrid plant needs a connection substation. These are usually procured privately, and that is TBEA's best first door.

**Script:**
"Every wind, solar or hybrid-storage plant in Türkiye collects power at 33 kilovolts and connects to the TEİAŞ grid at 154 or 400 kilovolts. The connection substation is usually bought by the developer or its EPC contractor, not by public tender. The buyer decides on price, delivery time, quality and service. With 120 gigawatts to build and a 33-gigawatt storage pipeline, the volume is real. And with the global transformer shortage, delivery slots matter. The potential TBEA scope is power transformers, GIS or AIS switchgear, instrument transformers, MV switchgear, protection, automation and SVG. What it takes: equipment that meets TEİAŞ specifications, type tests and references in order, EPC partners, and local after-sales."

**Detailed talking points (major slide):**
- Türkiye uses 154 kV, not 110 or 132 kV. TBEA's 170 kV-class GIS, CT/VT and 154 kV transformer designs need to be checked against TEİAŞ specifications. This is a concrete first technical work item.
- Connection substations are often transferred to TEİAŞ after commissioning, which is why TEİAŞ specifications apply even when the buyer is private.

**Likely TBEA question:** "Is TBEA equipment already accepted by TEİAŞ?"
**Suggested response:** "That is exactly what we need to establish together, product by product. We propose starting with the priority families: 154 kV power transformers, 170 kV GIS and instrument transformers. We will map the TEİAŞ requirements; TBEA provides the type tests and references."

**Transition:** "The second priority route is internationally financed."

## Slide 29 — World Bank and IFI-financed transmission packages

**Main message:** World Bank financing (about $748m for TEİAŞ, possibly growing much larger) brings international competitive bidding, where TBEA's references count.

**Script:**
"In 2025 the World Bank approved about 748 million dollars for TEİAŞ under the Transforming Power Transmission System Project. It pays for new and modernised high-voltage substations, lines, underground cables, control systems and preparation for HVDC. It is the first phase of a 1.5 billion dollar programme, and talks on up to six billion have been reported. These contracts follow World Bank procurement rules, which means open international competitive bidding. An earlier World Bank project already included GIS substations. Here, bidder experience, type tests and financial strength decide. Those are TBEA's strengths. Our role is tender tracking, local EPC alliances, documentation and logistics."

**Detailed talking points (major slide):**
- Domestic preference in World Bank contracts applies only if the bidding documents include it. We will check the actual P508354 bidding documents.
- Phase-one packages may already be in procurement. The bigger opportunity may be the next phases, so being prepared early matters.
- TBEA has World Bank and ADB-financed references elsewhere. Ask TBEA which ones can be presented.

**Likely TBEA question:** "We can see World Bank tenders ourselves. Why do we need a partner?"
**Suggested response:** "You can certainly see the published tender. What a local partner adds is earlier: knowing the package scope before it is published, forming the right consortium with a Turkish EPC, understanding how TEİAŞ evaluates, and supporting site, logistics and after-sales commitments. In practice, foreign bidders without a strong local partner often lose on these points."
