# Türkiye Electricity & Grid Investment Cycle: Verified Data Pack
Prepared 16 November 2026 for a TBEA board presentation (transformers, GIS/switchgear, cables, inverters/PCS, BESS, automation)

## How this was researched (please read first)
- All figures come from web search, run in standard and extended modes. **Direct page fetching (WebFetch) was blocked in this session.** The sandbox could not resolve enerji.gov.tr, teias.gov.tr, projects.worldbank.org, aa.com.tr, dailysabah.com or developmentaid.org. The figures below were therefore checked against search-engine extracts of those pages, not read from the full pages. Where a primary source (the Ministry, World Bank, TEİAŞ, EPDK or a KAP filing) is quoted through a secondary outlet, the outlet is named.
- **Before printing, open the primary URLs listed and check each number.** This applies most to anything marked Medium or Low.
- Confidence scale: **High** means an official figure that several independent outlets agree on, or that adds up internally. **Medium** means one good source, or small differences between sources. **Low** means a single secondary source, a forecast, or sources that disagree.

## 5. Energy storage

**Storage-linked pipeline**
- About **33 to 33.5 GW** of storage-integrated wind and solar pre-licences and licences, mostly from the 2022 to 2023 "storage-integrated" application window.
  - Rule: storage capacity must equal the plant's installed capacity.
  - Bayraktar has said "33,500 MW in investors' hands; only a very small part realised; 2,000 MW will come online in 2026."
  - Ember: 37 GWh in total, an average duration of about **1.1 hours**.
  - Ember says this pipeline is larger than any EU country's.

**Installed battery storage**
- **About 208 MW** of storage-integrated wind and solar commissioned as of February 2026 (Ember). Euronews, citing Ember, gives 0.21 GW.
- No later official installed-BESS figure was found for mid or late 2026.
- The government expects **2 to 3 GW** to be commissioned from 2026. That is a forecast, not an outturn.

**Targets**
- National Energy Plan (2022): **7.5 GW of batteries (2-hour) by 2035.** Some sources add an interim 2.1 GW by 2030.
- **Milli Enerji 2040 (5 November 2026): 140 GWh of battery storage plus 6 GW of pumped hydro.**
- A storage master plan is planned for 2026 (Bayraktar).

**Rules in 2026**
- EPDK storage amendments took effect on 1 January 2026:
  - unlicensed generators may add storage;
  - storage inside organised industrial zones (OIZs) is treated as consumption-integrated storage;
  - clearing rules changed.
- A further EPDK package was published around 17 to 19 October 2026:
  - consumers may install storage above their contracted capacity;
  - licence rules for storage-integrated plants changed.
- No independent (stand-alone) storage capacity auction was found.

## 6. Transmission grid investment and HVDC

**To 2035 (Bayraktar, 16 September 2026, via AA)**
- Total generation and transmission investment of about **$108bn**: about $80bn generation and **$28bn transmission.**
- **15,000 km of new AC transmission lines.**
- **HVDC corridors of about 9,580 km and 28 GW.** This replaces the October 2024 statement of 14,700 km and 40 GW, which also appears in the World Bank PID. The two figures differ; use the 2026 one and note the change.

**To 2040 (Milli Enerji 2040)**
- About $36bn for transmission.
- High-voltage lines grow from about **77,000 km to 112,000 km.**
- Transformer capacity grows from **about 200 GW to 316 GW.** This is probably MVA/GVA of TEİAŞ transformation capacity.
- Confidence: Medium.

**Existing grid (2005 to 2025 change, Ministry)**
- TEİAŞ substations: 512 → **824**.
- Transmission transformers: 1,096 → **2,215**.
- Line length: 43,000 → **77,000 km**.

**TEİAŞ 2026 budget and revenue caps**
- The 2026 public investment programme (KİT table) lists about **TL 60.2bn** for TEİAŞ. The units were not labelled in the extract, so confidence is Low.
- EPDK 2026 TEİAŞ revenue caps: system use about TL 95.9bn; system operation about TL 29.7bn.

**HVDC status**
- No EPC tender has been issued yet.
- TEİAŞ has awarded consultancy work for a generic **±525 kV, 2,000 MW VSC bipole** HVDC specification and an Owner's Engineer specification.
- The World Bank TPTS project (P508354) funds technical assistance for Türkiye's first HVDC corridors.
- **TEİAŞ and Hitachi Energy signed an HVDC MoU at COP31 in Antalya on 10 November 2026.**
- Existing link: 600 MW Van–Khoy (Iran) back-to-back HVDC; operating contract signed February 2024.

**Interconnections**
- Operational cross-border capacity is about **1.35 GW import / 2.6 GW export** (Ember, November 2026).
- Bulgaria net transfer capacity: 434 MW import / 334 MW export.
- Greece net transfer capacity: 216 MW import / 166 MW export.
- A second Türkiye–Greece line is expected in 2031.
- Türkiye earned about **$500m from electricity exports in 20 months.**
- **Azerbaijan–Georgia–Türkiye–Bulgaria Green Energy Corridor agreement signed at COP31** (November 2026).

## 8. Data centres

**Official target**
- Industry and Technology Minister Kacır, in the **2026–2030 AI Action Plan**: **at least 1 GW of data-centre capacity by 2030** and **over $10bn of investment.**
- The HIT-30 incentive scheme includes a $1.5bn data-centre call and a $1.6bn AI/cloud call.
- Confidence: **Medium-High.**

**Current capacity: estimates conflict**
- About 250 MW (sector and EY framing: "quadruple from 250 MW to 1 GW by 2030").
- About 160 MW operational at end-2025 (ResearchAndMarkets).
- About 66 to 120 MW of commercial colocation IT load (other market reports).
- The differences come from definitions (IT load versus facility load; colocation versus enterprise).
- Confidence: **Low.** On slides use "about 250 MW today versus a 1 GW 2030 target."

**Major announced projects, 2025–2026**
- **Rönesans and Digital Realty JV:**
  - groundbreaking about 19 to 20 October 2026, Başkent OIZ, Ankara;
  - phase 1 is **22 MW** of IT load, completing in 2028;
  - **$4.1bn** fixed investment across all phases (Kacır);
  - JV target of 200 MW across Ankara and Istanbul.
- **Turkcell and Google Cloud:**
  - first hyperscale cloud region in Türkiye, expected 2028–2029;
  - Turkcell's programme is about $3bn; Google's investment is about $2bn over a decade;
  - three new sites with 54 MW under construction;
  - Turkcell's existing footprint is about 41.5 MW.
- **Trendyol (Alibaba group) with Castle Investments:** about $500m for a 48 MW data centre in Ankara.
- **Khazna (G42, UAE):** up to 100 MW in Başkent OIZ, Ankara.
- **Türksat Gölbaşı** "digital fortress": phase 1 in Q1 2028.
- **AWS:** Istanbul Local Zone is live. This is not a full region.
- **Türk Telekom:** expanding sites in Ankara and Istanbul.
- No Microsoft or Oracle Türkiye region was found.

## 9. Industrial electrification and OIZs

**Number of OIZs**
- The Ministry of Industry (Kacır) cited **374** OIZs in 2026, up from 191 in 2002. Earlier speeches cited 362 to 373.
- OSBÜK (the OIZ federation) cited **418** as of March 2026. That count probably includes zones still being planned or built.
- Confidence: **Medium.** Use "about 370 to 420 OIZs" or cite the Ministry's 374.

**OIZs as distribution operators**
- Under EPDK's *Regulation on Electricity Market Activities of OIZs and Industrial Zones*, an OIZ may hold a **distribution licence and/or generation licence** and run its own distribution network and substations within its borders. Where an OIZ has no licence, the regional distribution company (EDAŞ) serves it.
- EPDK set **2026 distribution tariffs for 218 OIZs** (Official Gazette, 28 December 2025). This is the best proxy for the number of OIZs operating their own distribution networks.
- From 1 January 2026, storage inside OIZs counts as consumption-integrated storage. The 2026 ancillary-services rules require licensed OIZs to follow TEİAŞ emergency instructions.
- TEİAŞ is building dedicated 154 kV substations for OIZs (example: Nizip OIZ, with urgent expropriation decided in 2026).
- **Why this matters for TBEA:** OIZs are a semi-private buyer segment (the OIZ management buys its own 154/34.5 kV substations, MV switchgear, transformers and cables). This segment is often outside the state procurement law, so the domestic-preference rules do not apply automatically.

**EV electrification**

- Charging points: **47,908** (27,044 AC, 20,864 DC), up from 12,084 at the start of 2024 and 26,462 at the start of 2025.
- **July 2026 charging consumption: 89,947 MWh, +97% y/y** (EPDK data).

## 11. Domestic preference and procurement rules

**Turkish state tenders (Law 4734)**
- Under the Goods Procurement Implementing Regulation (art. 61), contracting authorities **may grant domestic bidders offering domestic goods (with a "Yerli Malı Belgesi") a price advantage of up to 15%.**
- For goods on the Ministry of Industry's mandatory list, a **15% advantage is compulsory.**
- Domestic-goods certification follows the *Yerli Malı Tebliği* (latest amendment SGM-2026/3, Official Gazette 7 February 2026).
- A law-firm commentary reports procedural amendments to national preference effective **1 January 2026**. Not verified against the Official Gazette.
- **Usage data:** in H1 2026, of 8,927 tenders open to foreign bidders, **3,771 applied a domestic price advantage**, mostly at 15% (Public Procurement Authority half-year report via Alomaliye).
- Separately, YEKA and YEKDEM renewables carry local-content premiums. The Domestic Components Regulation was amended on 13 December 2025.
- Sources:
  - Regulation art. 61: https://www.lexpera.com.tr/mevzuat/yonetmelikler/mal-alimi-ihaleleri-uygulama-yonetmeligi-2009-27159-1-2/36
  - Public Procurement Authority H1 2026: https://www.alomaliye.com/2026/09/01/2026nin-ilk-yarisinda-kamu-alimlari-1-95-trilyon-tlye-ulasti/
  - Yerli Malı Tebliği: https://www.lexpera.com.tr/mevzuat/tebligler/yerli-mali-tebligi-sgm-2024-10/3
  - Law-firm commentary: https://globallawexperts.com/public-procurement-lawyers-turkey/
  - Mondaq (Domestic Components Regulation): https://webiis10.mondaq.com/turkey/renewables/1726216/amendments-to-the-regulation-on-domestic-components
- Confidence: **High** for the 15% rule. **Medium** for the 2026 changes.

**What this means in practice**
- TEİAŞ's own-budget transformer and reactor tenders are regularly won by domestic makers. Examples:
  - Astor came first in TEİAŞ power transformer, autotransformer and shunt-reactor tenders worth TL 1.83bn in March 2026.
  - In April 2025, Astor won 34 x 250 MVA autotransformers, 30 power transformers and 14 shunt reactors, worth over TL 3bn.
  - Europower won TEİAŞ tenders of TL 704.5m and TL 987m.
  - Sources: https://www.getmidas.com/midasin-kulaklari-ow/astor-enerji-183-milyar-tllik-teias-ihalesinde-birinci-oldu-p-359281 and https://www.borsagundem.com.tr/astor-enerji-teiastan-3-milyar-tllik-transformator-ve-reaktor-ihalesini-kazandi

**World Bank-financed procurement**
- These contracts follow the **World Bank Procurement Regulations for IPF Borrowers** (current edition: 7th, September 2025), not Law 4734.
- High-value goods are normally bought through **open international competitive procurement**, open to bidders from all eligible countries, including China.
- The Bank's own rules allow a **domestic preference margin of 15% for goods**, but only if the bidding document says so. Historically it has rarely been used.
- Check the P508354 procurement plan and each bidding document to see whether domestic preference applies.
- Sources:
  - Procurement Regulations, 7th edition: https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/c84273d1b230aeb2b0b8134de5dc8cd7-0290012025/original/Procurement-Regulations-7th-Edition-Sep-2025.pdf
  - Center for Global Development (CGD) paper on local content in multilateral development bank (MDB) procurement: https://www.cgdev.org/sites/default/files/role-mdb-local-content-rules-industrial-strategy.pdf
- Confidence: **High** that WB rules apply. **Medium** on the exact domestic-preference paragraph in the 2025 edition.

1. **127.4 GW** installed capacity (end-September 2026), up from 122.5 GW at end-2025. **63% renewable; solar 28.2 GW** is now larger than gas (25.0 GW). *Ministry, October 2026.*
