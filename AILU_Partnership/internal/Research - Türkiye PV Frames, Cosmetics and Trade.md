**Implication for AILU's composite frame (analysis, L-M):**
- The definitive AD measure is worded as **aluminium** frames. A GF-PU composite frame is a different product and is very likely outside the measure's scope. That is AILU's strongest single talking point: Chinese aluminium frames now carry roughly 38–46% AD on top of normal duties, and AILU's frame might not.
- **Risks:**
  1. Classification. The AD is keyed to a national 8541.90 sub-line. Get a binding tariff information (BTI/"bağlayıcı tarife bilgisi") ruling from Turkish Customs before the first shipment.
  2. Scope or anti-circumvention action, if Turkish extruders lobby. Türkiye has used anti-circumvention against modules.
  3. Normal duty and İGV on whatever line the frame falls under.
- **Free zones and inward processing:** many Turkish module exporters operate in free zones or under inward processing (DİR). How the frame AD applies there was **not verified**. Ask a Turkish customs broker.

## D. Summary: opportunities and barriers

**Opportunities**
1. **Composite frames as an AD-free alternative (biggest angle).** Chinese aluminium frames now face definitive AD of 38.26–45.99% of CIF (since 24 Jun 2026) on top of base duty and İGV. Before that, China supplied about 76% of Turkish frame imports. Aina New Energy's GF-PU frame is a different material and likely falls outside the measure. Türkiye has about 13 GW of module capacity, mostly export-oriented and now partly US-facing. (H for the duty; L-M that composite is out of scope.)
2. **Coastal, desert and floating projects** (e.g., the YEKA floating zone at Demirköprü), where corrosion resistance matters.
3. **Localisation or JV route.** A pultrusion line in Türkiye (or a licence to a Turkish extruder or module maker) would make frames count as domestic for YEKA (75% module domestic contribution) and the YEKDEM bonus. It would also defuse AD and circumvention risk.
4. **Cosmetics: pumps, sprayers and complex closures.** China already supplies most of Türkiye's 9616 imports, and the export-led Turkish cosmetics sector (US$2.33bn exports, +11.8%) needs a lot of dispensing components.

**Barriers**
1. **Trade defence trend:** AD on frames, junction boxes (under investigation), solar glass (under investigation), modules (US$20–25/m²), BOPP and PET. Expect scrutiny and possible circumvention or scope requests from Turkish extruders (Arslan, Lazer, Pantech, Anka, CW Enerji).
2. **İGV of up to 48% on 4,344 non-FTA lines**, including aluminium. Exact rates for the HS lines in question are still unverified.
3. **Local-content rules** penalise imported frames in YEKA and YEKDEM-bonus projects.
4. **Bankability of composite frames:** limited field history, uncertain fire, UV, creep and recycling performance, and modules must be re-certified with the new frame (IEC 61215/61730). Turkish module makers selling to the US or EU will need lender and insurer acceptance.
5. **Cosmetics:** Türkiye is a net exporter of plastic packaging with strong local and multinational converters (ALPLA, Rosti, etc.), so commodity bottles and jars are hard to win. AILU's public profile shows no cosmetics-packaging track record.

**Questions for tomorrow**
- AILU: GF-PU frame certifications (IEC 61215/61730 on which module BOMs; TÜV/UL; fire class; salt mist; UV). Shipment volumes and reference customers. Price per frame or per W versus aluminium. Capacity. HS code they declare. Willingness to localise or license in Türkiye.
- Turkish side: current frame source and price (local versus Chinese with AD). Share of output going to export, US, YEKA or YEKDEM. Free-zone or DİR status. Customs broker view on classification and a BTI ruling for composite frames.
- Cosmetics: what exactly AILU makes in-house (pumps? tubes? jars?) versus traded goods, and the MOQs and lead times.
