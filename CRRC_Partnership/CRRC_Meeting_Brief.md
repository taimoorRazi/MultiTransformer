# CRRC Meeting Brief, Zhuzhou — Ulusoy Delegation

**Classification:** Ulusoy internal. Do not hand this document to CRRC.
**Meeting:** Ulusoy × CRRC senior executives, Zhuzhou, China, November 2026
**Deck:** Ulusoy - CRRC Partnership (59 slides). Script: CRRC_Speaker_Notes.md and the rehearsal PDF.
**How the meeting came about:** Cem Bey's senior-level contact with CRRC executives, building on the ongoing wind project with CRRC Wind in Türkiye.

---

## 1. Meeting objective

**One sentence:** Move from one project with CRRC Wind to an agreed framework for developing the regional market together.

What a good outcome looks like, in order of importance:
1. CRRC names a **contact team** (wind first, ideally solar and storage too) for Türkiye and the region.
2. CRRC agrees to a **working session** within about a month (İstanbul or Zhuzhou).
3. CRRC accepts the **registration principle** in general terms. The details come later.
4. CRRC agrees to review **the North Macedonia wind projects** (127 MW) and the **Bulgarian storage** under NDA.
5. CRRC gives a first view on **which turbine platform** it would lead with in Türkiye.

What we do **not** need today: exclusivity, a signed agreement, prices or commission.

### The Zhuzhou angle
- Zhuzhou is home to the **CRRC Zhuzhou Institute**, CRRC's power-electronics and new-energy base, which includes the wind business, IGBTs, converters and solar and storage products. Times Electric (listed) is part of this family.
- Expect wind engineers and power-electronics specialists. They will respect **precise technical questions** more than general enthusiasm. Slides 38–39 (North Macedonia turbines) and 44–45 (55 °C off-grid, grid-forming PCS) are the natural places for dialogue.
- CRRC knows Türkiye already through rail: Ankara metro cars (2012 contract), the **CRRC-MNG plant in Ankara**, and the İstanbul M11 airport-metro cars (60% local content). Mention it as a compliment.

## 2. Five messages CRRC must remember

1. **We already work together.** The ongoing wind project is the foundation; this is the next step, not a cold introduction.
2. **Türkiye is a large and growing wind market** (record 2.1 GW in 2025, 63 GW targeted by 2040), and the private segment is open now.
3. **The first Turkish reference is everything.** Our ongoing project can be that reference.
4. **Ulusoy brings accounts before the tender**: origination, senior access, EPCs, logistics and service coordination.
5. **A staged, protected model**: market development → market execution → localization, each earned by results. No exclusivity demanded today, no factory on day one.

---

## 3. Five things we want from CRRC

1. A named contact team (wind, solar, storage) with decision authority for our region.
2. A working-session date.
3. Agreement in principle to a project-registration mechanism.
4. A technical review of PLAKE, Makedonski Brod, Radilovo–Katselovo, Karnobat and the off-grid project.
5. A turbine-platform view for Türkiye and the Balkans (D175 / D195, and what fits the 5.5 MW Makedonski Brod class).

Ask for these as **suggestions** (slide 58), not demands.

## 4. Sensitive topics to avoid

**Never say in the room:**
- **The name of the ongoing project, its site, customer, capacity, price or schedule.** Call it "our ongoing wind project with CRRC Wind in Türkiye". If CRRC names it, follow their lead, but do not add details.
- Individual decision-makers, private accounts or relationship strength in Türkiye.
- Commission, margins, exclusivity terms or our negotiation position.
- Internal probability scoring or our research method.
- Competitor pricing.
- Any suggestion that CRRC is selected on any other project. Use "potential CRRC supply scope".

**Handle carefully if raised:**
- **The Ankara metro offset.** Public reports say a later complaint alleged that the 51% local-content commitment on the 2012 Ankara contract was not met. Do not raise it. If CRRC raises localization concerns, focus on M11 (60% local, delivered).
- **The EU foreign-subsidy review.** The EU has an in-depth FSR review of Goldwind (since Feb 2026), and EU anti-dumping duties on Chinese steel wind towers have applied since 2021. This matters for Bulgaria, not for Türkiye, North Macedonia or Serbia. Present it as "a factor to manage", never as a barrier.
- **The China–Türkiye MoU (US$15bn, 7 GW, 12 Nov 2026).** It is non-binding and names no companies. Do not guess who is behind it. If CRRC is involved, let them say so.
- **Company name confusion.** **Ulusoy Elektrik (ULUSE)** is an MV switchgear maker controlled by **Eaton** since 2019; it is **not** our group. If CRRC's people have searched online, they may ask. Answer clearly: "Ulusoy Elektrik was sold to Eaton in 2019. We are the Ulusoy family group."
- **Ulusoy Un** owns about 9.8 MW of unlicensed wind in Manisa for self-supply. Use it only if asked about wind experience; do not overstate it.
- **CRRC's own figures.** Slide 32 uses CRRC's figures (300+ wind farms, 9,000 turbines, 98% availability, 70 GW+ inverters). Let CRRC correct them. We removed the Italy Foggia 225 MW and the US$9.68bn revenue figures because they could not be verified.

## 5. Likely objections

| Objection | Where it comes from |
|---|---|
| "We already have an international sales team." | Normal reaction of a large OEM |
| "YEKA needs local content. Türkiye is closed to us." | They know the 55-point rule |
| "Goldwind and Envision are already there." | Competitive awareness |
| "Turkish buyers prefer European turbines." | Market share facts (Nordex 34%, Enercon 24%) |
| "Why should we protect your registrations?" | Fear of channel conflict and lock-in |
| "Let's finish the current project first, then talk." | Caution |
| "Give us customer names now." | Testing whether the pipeline is real |
| "Is the Bulgarian €185/MWh offer real?" | They may know power prices |

## 6. Suggested responses

- **Sales team:** "Your team is strong where CRRC is established. In Türkiye, wind buyers are private Turkish groups who decide through relationships, references and trust in service. We are a local market-development arm for your team, not a replacement for it."
- **YEKA:** "Agreed, YEKA is for later. The private market, capacity extensions, repowering and storage-linked licences are open now and have no local-content points. YEKA becomes possible in stage three, with local towers, blades and nacelle assembly."
- **Goldwind and Envision:** "They show Chinese turbines are accepted. Goldwind's share is still only about 2%. A strong first reference and a service presence matter more than being first."
- **European preference:** "That is the installed base, built over fifteen years. New buyers decide on bankability, delivery slots and price. The incumbents' order books are full, so delivery slots are a real argument."
- **Registration:** "It protects CRRC too: one channel per account, no two CRRC teams competing for the same customer. And it is activity-based. If we stop developing an account, the protection ends."
- **Finish the project first:** "Yes, completing it well is our first priority, and it is stage one of the model. But developers decide 12–24 months before turbine delivery. If we start the next projects only after completion, we lose a year."
- **Customer names:** "We will bring a first list to the working session once the registration principle is agreed. Today we have shown the projects we can already discuss under NDA."
- **€185/MWh:** "It is a three-year offtake offer the developer received. Bulgarian day-ahead prices were about €166/MWh in September and €232/MWh in October 2026, so it is market-related. The developer is reviewing the terms."

## 7. Questions we should ask CRRC

1. Which turbine platform would CRRC lead with in Türkiye: D175 (6.25–8.5 MW) or D195 (7.15 MW)? What wind classes do they cover?
2. What is the status of type certification for the D175, and which certificates do lenders in Europe and Türkiye already accept?
3. What delivery lead time could CRRC offer for 2027–2028 installations?
4. How does CRRC plan to provide service in a new market: own team, partner, or both?
5. Is CRRC open to local tower and blade sourcing in Türkiye, and has it looked at Turkish suppliers?
6. Who in CRRC owns the Balkans and the Middle East, for wind and for storage?
7. Can the 2.5 MW grid-forming PCS run an island system at 55 °C ambient?
8. How does CRRC usually work with local partners in its other export markets (Kazakhstan, Vietnam)?
9. What does CRRC need from us to get comfortable with a registration mechanism?

---

## 8. Negotiation points NOT to settle in the first meeting

- Exclusivity (by product, region or segment)
- Commission, fee structure or margin sharing
- Registration period length and renewal rules
- Pricing levels, payment terms, warranties or LDs
- Service contract structure and who employs the service team
- Any localization or JV commitment
- Ownership of customer relationships after delivery

If pushed: "These are important. We suggest preparing a draft together for the working session."

---

## 9. Recommended meeting closing

1. Summarise in three lines: we already work together; the private market is open now; a staged, protected model.
2. Restate the three requests: a contact team, a working-session date and agreement in principle to registration.
3. Ask directly but politely: "Would it be possible to agree today on a contact person and a date for the working session?"
4. Thank the hosts again, most senior first. Thank those who made the meeting possible (Cem Bey's counterparts).
5. Exchange gifts if appropriate, and photos.
